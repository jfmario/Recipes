# Beef Queso

This version is written for 8 generous appetizer portions as a dip (or fewer people if it is the centerpiece of nachos). Instead of process cheese, the sauce is built on a butter-and-flour roux so real block cheese melts in smoothly instead of turning greasy or grainy. The dip comes together on the stove in one skillet; keep it warm over low heat and stir occasionally, since a roux-based cheese sauce can separate if it sits too long unattended or over high heat. Check your taco seasoning label if you need to avoid gluten or specific allergens; brands vary.

## Ingredients

* 1 lb ground beef
* 1 (10 oz) can diced tomatoes with green chiles
* 2 tbsp butter
* 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
* 1 cup milk
* 2 cups shredded cheddar cheese
* 1 cup shredded Monterey Jack cheese

## Steps

* Set a large skillet on the counter and gather a wooden spoon or heatproof spatula, plus a whisk. Open 1 (10 oz) can diced tomatoes with green chiles, pour the contents into a strainer over the sink, and let the liquid drain while you shake the strainer gently once or twice. If you are not using pre-shredded cheese, shred enough cheddar and Monterey Jack to measure 2 cups and 1 cup.
* Crumble 1 lb ground beef into the skillet and set it over medium-high heat. Cook, breaking up the meat with the spoon, until no pink remains and the beef is lightly browned in spots, about 6 to 8 minutes. If more than a thin layer of fat pools in the pan, tilt the skillet and spoon off the excess so the dip does not taste greasy.
* Sprinkle 2 tbsp taco seasoning over the beef and stir for about 1 minute until the spices smell toasted and the meat is evenly coated. Push the beef to one side of the skillet.
* Add 2 tbsp butter to the cleared side of the skillet and let it melt. Whisk in 2 tbsp flour and cook, whisking constantly, for about 1 minute until the raw flour smell is gone and the mixture is light golden. Slowly whisk in 1 cup milk, then stir the roux together with the beef. Reduce the heat to medium-low.
* Add the drained tomatoes, then add the shredded cheddar and Monterey Jack a handful at a time, stirring until each addition melts before adding more. Once the sauce is smooth, keep it over medium-low to low heat; if it ever looks greasy or breaks, remove from the heat and whisk in a splash more milk to bring it back together. Taste and adjust salt only if needed (many taco seasonings are already salty). Serve immediately while hot, with tortilla chips or over nachos. If you like, top with sliced jalapeños, chopped cilantro, or a spoonful of salsa.
